Finite-difference operators

To solve a PDE in the TT format, we need the derivatives of a field as operators that act on its MPS and keep its low-rank structure. A one-dimensional derivative on a grid is a banded Toeplitz matrix, which has an exact representation as an MPO of small bond dimension in the QTT format (Kazeev & Khoromskij, 2012), and the operator in $N$ dimensions is the Kronecker sum of the one-dimensional ones, each acting on the sites of its own axis. This page explains which function builds what, and where the boundary conditions enter. The signatures are in the API reference.

Stencils and closures

The derivatives use central stencils of order $q = 2$ (three points) or $q = 8$ (nine points), whose coefficients derivative_coefficients returns. In the interior, the operator is the stencil scaled by $1/h^m$ for a derivative of order $m$. Next to a boundary, the stencil reaches $q/2$ ghost nodes, and the boundary condition decides what happens to them, as Grids, fields and boundary conditions describes: periodic sides wrap, Dirichlet and open sides truncate the stencil, and Neumann sides mirror it. The one-dimensional closed operator of an axis is built by onedim_differential_operator, and ndim_operator_mpo places it on the sites of its axis in the $N$-dimensional chain.

The operators of a field

DifferentialOpsScalar builds, for a scalar field and its grid, the first and second derivatives along every axis with the boundary conditions of that field, and DifferentialOps does the same for each component of a vector field. The default stencil order is 8. The functions gradient, divergence, advection and diffusion apply them to MPS.

The Laplacian has dedicated builders, because the linear systems of a flow solver are built from it:

  • kronecker_laplacian is the Kronecker sum of the second derivatives, $\Delta = \sum_i \partial_i^2$. Its bond dimension between axes is 2 (Kazeev & Khoromskij, 2012). The Neumann rows are not weighted, so it is the consistent operator for explicit terms, but it is not symmetric when a side is Neumann.
  • symmetrized_laplacian multiplies it by the Neumann weight, which gives a symmetric operator. We use it wherever CG solves the system, and weight the right-hand side the same way with symmetrize_rhs.
  • equicorrelated_laplacian is the operator $\mathcal{L}(\rho)$ of Sec. 5.1 of the paper, whose mixed derivatives couple every pair of axes at bond dimension 3, and which serves as the benchmark of the preconditioners.

Corrections of the right-hand side

With Dirichlet sides, the lift of the ghost values is part of the right-hand side of the linear system. The functions scalar_dirichlet_correction and divergence_dirichlet_correction build it for the pressure equation, and face_dirichlet_lift for a single face. The explicit time steppers do not impose the value of a Dirichlet side: BoundaryNullScalar and BoundaryNull zero the right-hand side on the rows next to it, so that those rows keep the values of the initial field.

Note

The derivation of every closure, with the dense matrices of a small grid, is in the boundary-condition tutorials (Velocity–pressure formulation and Vorticity–streamfunction formulation).
