Navier–Stokes

The incompressible Navier–Stokes equations couple the velocity $\mathbf{u}$ and the pressure $p$ through the constraint $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$, which makes the pressure a Lagrange multiplier that is determined by a Poisson equation at every time step. DifferenTT offers two formulations of the equations, with the same fields, operators, time steppers and linear solvers, and we describe here which type to pass and what each one imposes. The derivation of the boundary conditions of each formulation is in the corresponding tutorial (Velocity–pressure formulation and Vorticity–streamfunction formulation), and the exact rules are in the docstrings. The signatures are in the API reference.

Time steppers

An evolution is advanced by an explicit scheme, ExplicitEuler (first order in $dt$) or RK4 (fourth order), configured by a TimeEvolArgs with the initial and final times and the number of steps. timestep advances a state by one step given a right-hand side (AbstractRHS). The implicit steppers are a work in progress and are not part of the supported API.

Velocity–pressure formulation: Chorin projection

An INSExperiment holds the grid, the velocity (VectorField), the pressure (ScalarField), the Reynolds number and an optional obstacle mask (1 in the fluid, 0 in the obstacle). It follows the QTT pipeline of Peddinti et al. (2024) as used in Sec. 5.2 of the paper: the velocity is predicted by a time step of the momentum equation (NavierStokesPredictorRHS), the obstacle enters by multiplying the velocity by the mask, and the projection solves a Poisson equation for the pressure with the operator of symmetrized_laplacian and then subtracts its gradient. The fields live on a collocated grid, and the pressure system uses the compact Laplacian instead of the composition of the divergence and the gradient, so the projected velocity is only approximately divergence-free.

chorin_evolve! runs the evolution, taking any linear solver for the pressure system and any explicit stepper. The caches that it builds once (ChorinCache), the single step chorin_step, and the checks check_cfl and check_kolmogorov_scale are available separately. The boundary conditions are the types of Grids, fields and boundary conditions: in the setup of the paper the velocity has a Dirichlet inlet and a Neumann outlet, the pressure a Dirichlet outlet, and the second axis is periodic. For such an axis we recommend the grid Grid(arch, box_size; periodic = DifferenTT.periodic_axes(p_bcs)), which gives the stencils their intended period $L$ (the plain Grid gives $L + h$). The tutorial Velocity–pressure formulation derives the closures of this formulation, and Channel flow past a cylinder runs the pipeline of the paper in miniature.

Vorticity–streamfunction formulation

An INSVorticityExperiment evolves the vorticity $\omega$ and recovers the velocity from the stream function $\psi$, which solves a Poisson equation (poisson_streamfunction, streamfunction_velocity). The formulation has no pressure and its velocity is divergence-free by construction. Instead of boundary conditions on fields, each side of the domain is a VorticityFace that fixes the conditions of both $\psi$ and $\omega$: NoSlipWall, SlipWall, InflowFace, OutflowFace and PeriodicFace. vorticity_boundary_conditions derives the boundary conditions of the fields from the faces, vorticity_grid the grid, and cavity_faces the faces of the lid-driven cavity (Ghia, Ghia & Shin, 1982). The evolution is run by vorticity_evolve!. Walls and inflows are ghost nodes one spacing outside the stored nodes, which is why the grid of this formulation is not the default one. The derivation of every face is in the tutorial Vorticity–streamfunction formulation, and Lid-driven cavity runs the formulation on the classical benchmark.

Known limitations

The following limitations of v0.1 are recorded in the test suite, as @test_broken where a test applies, and in the release notes. They are planned for v0.2 and are not hidden by any option.

  • Mean removal in the Chorin projection. When the pressure has no Dirichlet side, its Poisson system is singular and the right-hand side is made consistent by subtracting a constant. That constant divides by $\prod_i (n_i - 1)$ instead of $\prod_i n_i$, so the regularized system is not exactly consistent. This is inactive when the pressure has a Dirichlet side, as in the channel of Sec. 5.2 of the paper.
  • Wall rows in the Chorin projection. The projection $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{u}^* - dt\, \nabla p$ is applied to every row, including the $q/2$ rows next to the Dirichlet velocity faces that the time steppers otherwise keep fixed, so the tangential pressure gradient changes the boundary velocity a little at every step.
  • Dirichlet closure with $q = 8$. The lift fills every ghost node beyond a Dirichlet side with the boundary value, which places the Dirichlet point one spacing beyond the last stored node and makes the closure only first-order accurate for $q = 8$ (second order for $q = 2$). This affects the outlet pressure of the Chorin projection.
  • Order of the Chorin step. chorin_step masks and projects the velocity it receives and then predicts, so the velocity that it returns, and that chorin_evolve! stores, is the provisional field $\mathbf{u}^*$ of the next step and not the projected one. The order predict, mask, project, which solves the same pressure systems, is to be reconsidered.
  • Period of a periodic axis. Grid without the periodic flag keeps a node at both ends of a periodic axis, so its stencils have period $L + h$ (Grids, fields and boundary conditions).
