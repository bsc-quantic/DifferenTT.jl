Tutorials

The tutorials are runnable Literate scripts, rendered into this site and executed during its build. Each one is also a standalone script, which runs with julia --project=examples examples/<file>.jl from the root of the repository. They use small grids so that every script takes under two minutes, and every random choice is seeded. We order them from the representation of a function to the incompressible flow solvers, and the first seven are meant to be read in sequence.

TutorialWhat it teaches
Functions in the QTT formatGrids and architectures (blocked, interleaved, reversed); encoding a function by cross interpolation; sample, samples and integrate; the behavior of bond dimensions.
The Poisson equation in one dimensionBoundary conditions and the finite-difference operator MPO; solving with ResidualALS; the error against the exact solution as the grid is refined.
The Poisson equation in N dimensionsPoisson in the TT format with $N = 5$ and $N = 20$ variables, one site of dimension $d = 10$ per variable; ranks; ALS versus AMEn.
Factorized approximate inverse preconditionersThe paper in miniature: why truncated direct inverses lose definiteness, how fai_preconditioner avoids it, the choice of $\mu$, and its effect inside ResidualALS.
Solver statisticsLocal iterations per site with KrylovSweepStats, local conditioning with KrylovCondStats and KrylovSpectrumStats.
Channel flow past a cylinderSec. 5.2 of the paper in miniature: a QTT channel with a cylinder mask, and the pressure Poisson solve with and without a reused FAI.
Lid-driven cavityThe vorticity–streamfunction formulation: its faces and a short cavity run, compared with the data of Ghia, Ghia & Shin (1982).

The section Boundary conditions contains two longer tutorials that derive, step by step, every boundary closure of the library, with the dense matrices of a small grid. The docstrings of the boundary-condition functions link to them, and so do the manual pages on Grids, fields and boundary conditions, Finite-difference operators and Navier–Stokes.

TutorialWhat it teaches
Velocity–pressure formulationThe closures of the operators of a field, the Laplacians and the pressure system of the Chorin projection: ghost nodes, lifts, the Neumann weight and the mean removal.
Vorticity–streamfunction formulationThe faces of the formulation, the grid they imply, the stream-function system, the wall vorticity and the closures of the transport of the vorticity.
