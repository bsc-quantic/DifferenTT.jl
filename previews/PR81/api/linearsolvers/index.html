Linear solvers

Local (DMRG-type) and global (Krylov) linear solvers in the TT format, preconditioners, and solver statistics.

Exported names and names declared public are listed here; the other names of the area are on the Internals page.

DifferenTT.AbstractDMRGSolver — Type
AbstractDMRGSolver <: AbstractLinearSolver

Supertype of the solvers that proceed in an alternating fashion along the tensor train: ALS, ResidualALS, AMEn and ExpandedALS.

At each step, we use the current solution guess, orthogonalized so that its orthogonality center lies on the sites being optimized, to project the global system onto a local problem $A_{\text{loc}} \mathbf{x}_{\text{loc}} = \mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}$ on one or two sites (Sec. 2.1, Fig. 2 of the paper). The local problem is solved by an AbstractUpdater, and the TT format is then recovered by an SVD that also sets the new bond dimension. Since the local solve is exact within the current subspace, its convergence is not affected by truncation errors, which is what makes these solvers robust for CG. Every concrete subtype has the fields sweep_kwargs::SweepingArgs, updater_kwargs::LocalUpdaterArgs and updater::AbstractUpdater.

DifferenTT.AbstractGlobalSolver — Type
AbstractGlobalSolver <: AbstractLinearSolver

Supertype of the solvers that run a Krylov method on the whole tensor train: GlobalCG and GlobalGMRES.

Their iterates are sums and operator products of tensor trains, whose bond dimensions are truncated after every operation in order to keep the cost bounded. These truncations perturb the Krylov recurrences: for CG in particular, they can destroy the conjugacy of the search directions, so convergence is not guaranteed when the truncation is severe (Sec. 2.1 of the paper). Every concrete subtype has the field updater_kwargs::GlobalUpdaterArgs.

DifferenTT.AbstractLinearSolver — Type
AbstractLinearSolver

Supertype of every solver of the linear system $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ in the TT format, where A is an MPO and $\mathbf{b}$ an MPS. Each concrete solver stores its own configuration and is run by linearsolve.

We distinguish two classes of solvers, as in Sec. 2.1 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format. The DMRG-type solvers (AbstractDMRGSolver) project the system onto one or two sites at a time and solve each local problem with a classical iterative method, while the global solvers (AbstractGlobalSolver) run a Krylov method on the whole tensor train and truncate its iterates.

DifferenTT.AbstractUpdater — Type
AbstractUpdater

Supertype of the local solvers of the DMRG-type solvers: KrylovUpdater (the default) and QRUpdater.

An updater is a callable (updater)(problem, init; internal_kwargs, coefficients, kwargs...). It receives the local problem problem (an ITensorMPS.ReducedLinearProblem, or its preconditioned counterpart), the current local tensor init as initial guess, and the fields of LocalUpdaterArgs as kwargs. It solves $(a_0 + a_1 A_{\text{loc}}) \mathbf{x}_{\text{loc}} = \mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}$, with coefficients = (a₀, a₁) (always (false, true) in linearsolve), and returns the new local tensor and a NamedTuple with an info entry.

DifferenTT.GlobalUpdaterArgs — Type
GlobalUpdaterArgs(; ishermitian=false, isposdef=false, krylovdim=30, maxiter=100, rtol=1e-8, verbosity=0,
                  maxdim=typemax(Int), cutoff=1e-8, orth_maxdim=typemax(Int), orth_cutoff=1e-12,
                  numiter_store=nothing, converged_store=nothing)

Configuration of a global solver (GlobalCG or GlobalGMRES), stored in its updater_kwargs field. linearsolve copies it into a TTCG or TTGMRES algorithm, and any field can be overridden at call time.

The two pairs of truncation parameters control the bond dimension of the iterates. Since every operator application and every sum of tensor trains increases the bond dimension, we truncate after each of them, and these truncations are the price paid for running a Krylov method on the whole tensor train (see AbstractGlobalSolver).

Fields

  • ishermitian::Bool=false, isposdef::Bool=false: properties of A. GlobalCG requires both to be true; GlobalGMRES does not read them.
  • krylovdim::Int=30: Krylov dimension of TT-GMRES before a restart (not read by TT-CG).
  • maxiter::Int=100: maximum number of TT-CG iterations, or of TT-GMRES restart cycles.
  • rtol::Float64=1e-8: relative tolerance; the solve stops at $\|\mathbf{b} - A\mathbf{x}\| \le \texttt{rtol}\, \|\mathbf{b}\|$, where the residual is the truncated one of the recurrence.
  • verbosity::Int=0: KrylovKit verbosity levels (1: warn on no convergence, 2: start and end, 3: every iteration).
  • maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), cutoff::Float64=1e-8: truncation of the operator applications and of the updates of $\mathbf{x}$ and of the residual.
  • orth_maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), orth_cutoff::Float64=1e-12: truncation of the new search direction (TT-CG) or of the Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization (TT-GMRES).
  • numiter_store=nothing: a Ref{Int} (or anything that supports store[] = value) that receives the number of iterations.
  • converged_store=nothing: a Ref{Bool} that receives whether the solve converged.

A solve that stops without converging always logs a warning, whether or not converged_store is set.

DifferenTT.LocalUpdaterArgs — Type
LocalUpdaterArgs(; ishermitian=false, isposdef=false, rtol=1e-8, krylovdim=30, maxiter=100, verbosity=0,
                 numiter_store=nothing, cond_store=nothing, pc_cond_store=nothing, spectrum_store=nothing,
                 spd_tol=nothing)

Configuration of the local solver of a DMRG-type solver (AbstractDMRGSolver), stored in its updater_kwargs field and read by its AbstractUpdater at every local problem.

The first six fields set the Krylov method of KrylovUpdater, which passes them to KrylovKit.linsolve. KrylovKit selects CG when ishermitian && isposdef, and restarted GMRES otherwise, so an SPD system must set both flags to be solved by CG. The other fields attach optional diagnostics. Each one has a single role and costs nothing when it is nothing, since the dense local spectrum, of cost $\mathcal{O}(n_{\text{loc}}^3)$ for a local block of dimension $n_{\text{loc}}$, is computed only when cond_store, pc_cond_store, spectrum_store or spd_tol asks for it. A local block that is not SPD raises no warning: it is detected only by spd_tol, which stops the solve, or recorded by spectrum_store.

Fields

  • ishermitian::Bool=false, isposdef::Bool=false: properties of the local operator, used to select CG.
  • rtol::Float64=1e-8: relative tolerance of the local solve. KrylovKit stops at $\|\mathbf{r}_{\text{loc}}\| \le \max(10^{-12}, \texttt{rtol}\, \|\mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}\|)$.
  • krylovdim::Int=30: Krylov dimension of GMRES before a restart.
  • maxiter::Int=100: maximum number of GMRES restarts. KrylovKit gives CG krylovdim * maxiter iterations (3000 by default).
  • verbosity::Int=0: KrylovKit verbosity; with verbosity > 0 we also log the local condition numbers we compute.
  • numiter_store=nothing: a KrylovSweepStats that records the iteration count of every local solve.
  • cond_store=nothing: a KrylovCondStats that records $\kappa(A_{\text{loc}})$ for every local problem.
  • pc_cond_store=nothing: a KrylovCondStats that records $\kappa(M^{-1}_{\text{loc}} A_{\text{loc}})$ for every local problem of a preconditioned solve, and $\kappa(A_{\text{loc}})$ for the local problems that preconditioner_sites leaves unpreconditioned, so that it stays aligned with cond_store.
  • spectrum_store=nothing: a KrylovSpectrumStats that records the sign-aware spectra of the local operator and preconditioner.
  • spd_tol::Union{Nothing,Float64}=nothing: if set, a local $A_{\text{loc}}$ or $M^{-1}_{\text{loc}} A_{\text{loc}}$ with an eigenvalue whose real part is below $-\texttt{spd\_tol} \max|\lambda|$ throws a NotSPDError. It requires CG.

The condition numbers are $\kappa = \max|\lambda| / \min|\lambda|$ of the dense local operator, which is the spectral condition number that bounds the convergence of CG and PCG. For GMRES it is only a heuristic indicator, because it is a lower bound on $\kappa_2$ for non-normal operators.

QRUpdater reads only verbosity, numiter_store and cond_store.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if spd_tol is set without ishermitian=true and isposdef=true.

DifferenTT.ResidualObserver — Type
ResidualObserver(; A=nothing, b=nothing, rtol=1e-8) <: ITensorMPS.AbstractObserver
ResidualObserver(A, b, rtol)

Observer that stops the sweeps of a DMRG-type solver once the global relative residual $\|A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}\| / \|\mathbf{b}\|$ falls below rtol. Pass it as observer! in SweepingArgs.

The local problems are solved to their own tolerance, but nothing in the sweeps controls the global residual, so without an observer ALS and ResidualALS always run nsweeps sweeps. We evaluate the residual at the end of every sweep, from the expansion

\[\|A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}\|^2 = \langle A\mathbf{x}, A\mathbf{x}\rangle - 2\,\mathrm{Re} \langle \mathbf{b}, A\mathbf{x}\rangle + \langle \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{b}\rangle,\]

which costs three TT inner products and never forms $A\mathbf{x}$. Note that this expansion suffers from cancellation: its rounding error is of order $\sqrt{\epsilon}\, \|\mathbf{b}\| \approx 1.5 \cdot 10^{-8} \|\mathbf{b}\|$, so a tolerance below about $10^{-8}$ is checked against rounding noise (#76).

A and b can be left as nothing: the DMRG-type linearsolve methods then bind them to the operator and right-hand side of each call (always the unpreconditioned ones, never M⁻¹), so that one observer stored in SweepingArgs can be reused across solves with different right-hand sides (see bind_observer). The global solvers do not use observers; they stop on their own rtol.

Fields

  • A: the operator, an MPO, or nothing.
  • b::Union{MPS,Nothing}: the right-hand side, or nothing.
  • rtol::Float64=1e-8: tolerance on the relative residual.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: from ITensorMPS.checkdone!, if A or b is still nothing when the residual is checked.

DifferenTT.SweepingArgs — Type
SweepingArgs(; nsweeps=5, maxdim=30, cutoff=1e-10, nsite=2, outputlevel=0,
             observer! = ITensorMPS.default_observer(), sweep_observer! = ITensorMPS.default_sweep_observer(),
             checkdone = default_differenTT_checkdone)

Configuration of the sweeps of a DMRG-type solver (AbstractDMRGSolver), stored in its sweep_kwargs field. linearsolve passes the fields to the sweeping loop as keywords, and any of them can be overridden at call time.

A sweep is one forward half-sweep, from the first site to the last, followed by one reverse half-sweep. The solve stops after nsweeps sweeps, or earlier when checkdone, evaluated at the end of every sweep, returns true. Note that the residual is therefore checked only once per sweep, so a solve that narrowly misses its tolerance pays for a whole extra sweep (Sec. 5.1 of Torrente et al.).

Fields

  • nsweeps::Int=5: maximum number of sweeps.
  • maxdim::Int=30: maximum bond dimension of the solution after each SVD.
  • cutoff::Float64=1e-10: SVD cutoff. ALS follows the ITensors convention and discards the smallest singular values whose relative weight $\sum_{k>r} \sigma_k^2 / \sum_k \sigma_k^2$ is at most cutoff. ResidualALS and AMEn choose the rank by the local residual instead, and use the cutoff, in the same convention, only as a floor on the rank.
  • nsite::Int=2: number of sites of the local problem. ALS accepts 1 or 2, but only the 2-site variant (MALS) can change the bond dimension. ResidualALS truncates by the residual only for nsite = 2; AMEn ignores it; ExpandedALS requires 1.
  • outputlevel::Int=0: printed output of the sweeping loop (1: one line per sweep).
  • observer!: observer updated after every local step, and checked by checkdone. A ResidualObserver stops the solve once the global relative residual is below its rtol.
  • sweep_observer!: observer updated after every sweep, and checked by checkdone.
  • checkdone: function of the keywords state, sweep, outputlevel, observer and sweep_observer that returns true to stop; the default is default_differenTT_checkdone.

DifferenTT.linearsolve — Function
linearsolve(solver::AbstractLinearSolver, A::MPO, b::MPS, x0::Union{MPS,Nothing}; kwargs...) -> MPS
linearsolve(solver::AbstractLinearSolver, A::MPO, b::MPS, M⁻¹::AbstractPreconditioner, x0::Union{MPS,Nothing};
            kwargs...) -> MPS

Solve $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ with solver, optionally preconditioned by M⁻¹, starting from x0.

The operator A must share its site indices with b, with prime level 0 for the input and 1 for the output. If x0 is nothing, we start from the zero MPS of bond dimension 1 (zeros_mps) on the sites of b. The second method applies the preconditioner M⁻¹, a Preconditioner (an approximate inverse) or a FactorizedPreconditioner (the factor $G_L$ of $M^{-1} = G_L^\top G_L$). The DMRG-type solvers apply it to the local problems, in the projected form of Sec. 4 of the paper, while the global solvers apply it to the whole residual.

The keywords given at call time override the solver's stored configuration for this call only, which lets one solver object be reused with different settings:

  • DMRG-type solvers (AbstractDMRGSolver): any field of SweepingArgs (nsweeps, maxdim, cutoff, nsite, outputlevel, observer!, sweep_observer!, checkdone), and the other keywords of ITensorMPS.alternating_update (e.g. mindim), except its time-evolution and disk options (time_step, write_when_maxdim_exceeds), which ITensorMPS does not support for linear systems; noise is accepted but ignored by ResidualALS and AMEn. An observer without an operator or right-hand side, such as ResidualObserver(rtol=1e-6), is bound to A and b of this call (see bind_observer). The preconditioned method also accepts preconditioner_sites, a collection of site indices in 1:length(b): only the local problems whose first site is in it are preconditioned, and the others are solved with A alone.
  • Global solvers (AbstractGlobalSolver): any field of GlobalUpdaterArgs (e.g. rtol, maxiter, maxdim). Other keywords are ignored.

Returns

  • MPS: the approximate solution $\mathbf{x}$.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if preconditioner_sites has an entry outside 1:length(b), or a ResidualObserver has no operator and right-hand side when it is checked.

Examples

A, sites = …                                  # SPD MPO, e.g. -1.0 * symmetrized_laplacian(field, grid)
b = random_mps(sites; linkdims=2)
solver = ResidualALS(;
    sweep_kwargs=SweepingArgs(; nsweeps=10, maxdim=50, (observer!)=ResidualObserver(; rtol=1e-6)),
    updater_kwargs=LocalUpdaterArgs(; ishermitian=true, isposdef=true, rtol=1e-8),
)
x = linearsolve(solver, A, b, nothing)
x = linearsolve(solver, A, b, fai_preconditioner(A; μ=1e7, solver=ResidualALS()), nothing; nsweeps=4)

See also ALS, ResidualALS, AMEn, ExpandedALS, GlobalCG, GlobalGMRES.

DifferenTT.FactorizedPreconditioner — Type
FactorizedPreconditioner(G_L::MPO) <: AbstractPreconditioner

Preconditioner $M^{-1} = G_L^\top G_L$ stored through its factor $G_L$ alone, as built by fai_preconditioner.

Since $\mathbf{v}^\top G_L^\top G_L \mathbf{v} = \|G_L \mathbf{v}\|^2 \ge 0$, the preconditioner is positive semidefinite by construction, and definite whenever $G_L$ is non-singular, whatever the truncation of $G_L$. We never form the product $G_L^\top G_L$, which would square the bond dimension and whose truncation would lose this property. Globally, we apply $G_L$ and then its transpose (transpose_mpo), each truncated as the iterates. Locally, a DMRG-type solver projects $G_L$ alone and applies the two layers $(U^\top G_L^\top U)(U^\top G_L U)$, at a cost relative to an operator of bond dimension $\chi_A$ of $1 + 2\chi_{G_L}/\chi_A$ (Sec. 4, Fig. 8 of Torrente et al.). The intermediate projection $U U^\top$ makes this local preconditioner differ from $U^\top G_L^\top G_L U$, but it is still positive semidefinite, and definite whenever $U^\top G_L U$ is non-singular.

Fields

  • operator::MPO: the factor $G_L$, with prime levels 0 (input) and 1 (output).

DifferenTT.Preconditioner — Type
Preconditioner(operator::MPO) <: AbstractPreconditioner

Preconditioner given by an approximate inverse $M^{-1} \approx A^{-1}$ stored as one MPO, with the site indices of A at prime levels 0 (input) and 1 (output).

Globally, GlobalCG and GlobalGMRES apply it to the residual with ITensorMPS.apply, truncated as the iterates. Locally, a DMRG-type solver projects it onto the sites of each local problem with the same basis $U$ as the operator, so that the local Krylov solver uses $U^\top M^{-1} U$ (Sec. 2.1 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format). For CG, $M^{-1}$ must be SPD. Note that a truncated approximate inverse controls the norm error but not the sign of its eigenvalues, so it can be indefinite (Fig. 3); FactorizedPreconditioner avoids this by construction.

The constructors rank1_preconditioner, newtoniter_preconditioner, linsolve_preconditioner, sincquadrature_preconditioner and inverse_laplacian_preconditioner return a Preconditioner.

Fields

  • operator::MPO: the approximate inverse $M^{-1}$.

DifferenTT.QRUpdater — Type
QRUpdater() <: AbstractUpdater

Local solver that solves each local problem exactly, by a dense QR factorization.

We contract the projected operator into a dense $n_{\text{loc}} \times n_{\text{loc}}$ matrix, where $n_{\text{loc}}$ is the dimension of the local tensor, and solve $(a_0 + a_1 A_{\text{loc}}) \mathbf{x}_{\text{loc}} = \mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}$ by QR, at a cost of $\mathcal{O}(n_{\text{loc}}^3)$. Since $n_{\text{loc}} = \chi^2 d^2$ for a 2-site problem with bond dimension $\chi$ and site dimension $d$, this is only affordable for small bond dimensions ($n_{\text{loc}} = 1024$ for $\chi = 16$ on QTT sites), where it serves as an exact reference for the Krylov solves. It works on unpreconditioned local problems only, and reads verbosity, numiter_store (which records one iteration per solve) and cond_store from LocalUpdaterArgs.

DifferenTT.ALS — Type
ALS(; sweep_kwargs=SweepingArgs(), updater_kwargs=LocalUpdaterArgs(), updater=KrylovUpdater()) <: AbstractDMRGSolver

Alternating Linear Scheme (ALS) for $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ in the TT format (Holtz, Rohwedder & Schneider, 2012), built on the sweeps of ITensorMPS.alternating_update.

At each step, we project the system onto sweep_kwargs.nsite adjacent sites, solve the local problem with updater, and split the new local tensor back into the tensor train by an SVD truncated to sweep_kwargs.maxdim and sweep_kwargs.cutoff. With nsite = 2, the default, this is the two-site variant also known as Modified ALS (MALS), whose SVD can increase or decrease the bond dimension of the solution. With nsite = 1 the bond dimension of x0 is kept, so the 1-site variant only converges if x0 already has enough rank; AMEn and ExpandedALS are the 1-site solvers that enrich the basis. For an SPD A, set ishermitian=true and isposdef=true in updater_kwargs so that the local problems, which are SPD as well, are solved by CG.

The truncation of ALS is controlled by the singular values alone. ResidualALS instead chooses the rank by its effect on the local residual, which is the variant used in the paper's experiments.

Fields

  • sweep_kwargs::SweepingArgs: sweeps, truncation and observers.
  • updater_kwargs::LocalUpdaterArgs: local Krylov solver and diagnostics.
  • updater::AbstractUpdater: local solver, KrylovUpdater or QRUpdater.

Examples

solver = ALS(;
    sweep_kwargs=SweepingArgs(; nsweeps=4, maxdim=16),
    updater_kwargs=LocalUpdaterArgs(; ishermitian=true, isposdef=true),
)
x = linearsolve(solver, A, b, nothing)

See also linearsolve, ResidualALS.

DifferenTT.ExpandedALS — Type
ExpandedALS(; sweep_kwargs=SweepingArgs(nsite=1), updater_kwargs=LocalUpdaterArgs(), expander_func=NullExpansion(),
            updater=KrylovUpdater()) <: AbstractDMRGSolver

One-site ALS whose basis is expanded between sweeps, in the spirit of the ALS(t+z) method of Dolgov & Savostyanov (2014).

The one-site ALS is cheaper per step than MALS, since its local problems have dimension $\chi^2 d$ instead of $\chi^2 d^2$, but it cannot change the bond dimension of the solution, so it stalls whenever the initial guess lacks rank. We therefore expand the tensor train before every sweep after the first, as long as its maximum bond dimension is below sweep_kwargs.maxdim: expander_func returns a larger tensor train, which we orthogonalize (and normalize, if normalize = true is passed to linearsolve) before the sweep. With ResidualExpansion, the added directions come from the residual, as in ALS(t+z); NullExpansion recovers the plain one-site ALS. Unlike AMEn, which enriches every bond within the sweep, the expansion here happens once per sweep and on the whole tensor train. Note that the expansion is not truncated, so each one adds the full bond dimension of the expanding tensor train, and that every sweep deep-copies the projected problem and rebuilds its environments from scratch.

Fields

  • sweep_kwargs::SweepingArgs: sweeps and observers; nsite must be 1.
  • updater_kwargs::LocalUpdaterArgs: local Krylov solver and diagnostics.
  • expander_func::AbstractALSExpansion: the expansion applied before each sweep after the first.
  • updater::AbstractUpdater: local solver.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if sweep_kwargs.nsite != 1.

Examples

solver = ExpandedALS(;
    sweep_kwargs=SweepingArgs(; nsite=1, nsweeps=6, maxdim=20),
    updater_kwargs=LocalUpdaterArgs(; ishermitian=true, isposdef=true),
    expander_func=ResidualExpansion(A, b; maxdim=4),
)
x = linearsolve(solver, A, b, nothing)

See also linearsolve, AMEn.

DifferenTT.RandomLTexpansion — Type
RandomLTexpansion(; rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng()) <: AbstractALSExpansion

Expansion that adds to the state the vectorization of a random lower-triangular operator, drawn from rng.

DifferenTT.RandomSPSDexpansion — Type
RandomSPSDexpansion(; rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng()) <: AbstractALSExpansion

Expansion that adds to the state the vectorization of a random symmetric positive semidefinite (SPSD) operator, drawn from rng.

It is meant for unknowns that are themselves vectorized operators on QTT sites, such as the approximate inverse $\tilde{G}_L$ of fai_preconditioner: we draw a random MPO $R$ with sites of dimension 2, form the SPSD $R R^\top$, and fuse its input and output indices into one index of dimension 4 per site. The state must therefore have sites of dimension 4. The result is the direct sum of the state and this tensor train, so it changes the state as well as its basis, and the next sweep re-solves the local problems from there.

DifferenTT.ResidualExpansion — Type
ResidualExpansion(A::MPO, b::MPS; cutoff=1e-8, maxdim=5) <: AbstractALSExpansion

Expansion that enriches the basis of the state with the residual $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{b} - A\mathbf{x}$, truncated to cutoff and maxdim, without changing the represented vector.

We form the direct sum of $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{r}$ after setting the first core of $\mathbf{r}$ to zero: the result still represents $\mathbf{x}$, but its bond spaces contain those of the residual, which are the directions that the next one-site sweep most needs, as in the ALS(t+z) method of Dolgov & Savostyanov (2014). Each expansion adds at most maxdim to every bond dimension.

Arguments

  • A::MPO, b::MPS: the system of the solve; A must act on the sites of b.

            residual_tol=1e-12, residual_damp=1.1) <: AbstractDMRGSolver

Two-site ALS (MALS) for $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ whose SVD truncation chooses the rank by the local residual, as proposed by Oseledets & Dolgov (2012). This is the solver called MALS in Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format (Sec. 5), where it proved more robust than ALS in meeting the prescribed residual bounds.

A truncation by singular values controls the error in $\mathbf{x}$, but not its effect on the residual, which is what the solve has to bring below a tolerance. We therefore choose the rank of each new pair of cores as follows. After the local solve, we compute the full SVD of the two-site tensor $\phi = U S V^\top$ and the relative local residual $\varepsilon_{\text{exact}}$ of the untruncated tensor. We then keep the smallest rank $r$ whose truncation $\phi_r$ satisfies

\[\frac{\|A_{\text{loc}}\phi_r - \mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}\|}{\|\mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}\|} \le \delta \max\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{N}}, \varepsilon_{\text{exact}}\right),\]

where $\varepsilon$ is residual_tol, $\delta$ is residual_damp and $N$ is the number of sites, found by a bisection over $r$ that assumes the residual decreases with the rank. The scaling by $1/\sqrt{N}$ follows Dolgov's implementations, so that the $N$ local errors of a sweep add up to about $\varepsilon$. The rank is then clamped to [mindim, maxdim]. The sweeping cutoff (from SweepingArgs) adds a floor on the rank: we keep at least the smallest rank whose discarded relative weight $\sum_{k>r} \sigma_k^2 / \sum_k \sigma_k^2$ is at most cutoff, as in ITensors, and cutoff = 0 disables the floor, so that the rank is chosen by the residual alone. In a preconditioned solve, the local residual is always that of the unpreconditioned $A_{\text{loc}}$.

The residual-based truncation applies only to nsite = 2. With nsite = 1, the sweep falls back to the truncation of ALS. The cost per bond is that of the local solve plus $\mathcal{O}(\log r_{\max})$ applications of $A_{\text{loc}}$ for the bisection.

  • updater::AbstractUpdater: local solver.

  • residual_damp::Float64=1.1: factor $\delta$ by which a truncated tensor may exceed the bound.

AMEn(; sweep_kwargs=SweepingArgs(), updater_kwargs=LocalUpdaterArgs(), updater=KrylovUpdater(),

Alternating Minimal Energy (AMEn) solver for $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ in the TT format (Dolgov & Savostyanov, 2014, Part II, arXiv:1304.1222).

The two-site MALS adapts the rank of the solution, but its local problems have dimension $\chi^2 d^2$, while the one-site ALS is cheaper but cannot change the rank. AMEn keeps the one-site local problems and adapts the rank by enriching each core with directions taken from the residual, so that the basis grows where the error is. At every bond of a sweep we proceed as follows:

  1. We update the core by a one-site local solve.
  2. We truncate the core on its own by the local residual, as in ResidualALS: we keep the smallest rank whose local residual stays below residual_damp * max(residual_tol / √N, ε_exact), where $N$ is the number of sites and $\varepsilon_{\text{exact}}$ the local residual of the untruncated core, with the sweeping cutoff as a Frobenius floor in the ITensors convention. Since the core is truncated alone, the rank does not depend on the scale of $\mathbf{x}$ nor on the enrichment.
  3. We enrich the truncated core with up to enrichment_maxdim directions of the residual of the two-site problem, unless that residual is already below kick_tol, so that the rank stops growing once the local problem is solved. The enriched core is orthonormalized with a QR, so the enrichment changes neither $\mathbf{x}$ nor depends on its scale.
  4. We move the orthogonality center to the next site.

The sweeping maxdim is honored: the truncation of step 2 leaves room for the enrichment of step 3. AMEn always sweeps with one-site updates, so SweepingArgs.nsite is ignored.

fai_preconditioner(A::MPO; μ::Real, solver::AbstractLinearSolver, totalmaxdim::Int=5, D_cutoff::Real=1e-15,

Build a factorized approximate inverse (FAI) preconditioner $M^{-1} = G_L^\top G_L \approx A^{-1}$ of the SPD operator A in the TT format.

Truncated TT approximate inverses control the norm error but not the sign of their eigenvalues, so they can become indefinite and break CG (Sec. 3 and Fig. 3 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format). We therefore build the inverse in factorized form, as in the FSAI of Kolotilina & Yeremin (1993): $G_L^\top G_L$ is positive semidefinite by construction, and definite whenever $G_L$ is non-singular, whatever the truncation of $G_L$. Since $G_L$ approximates the inverse $L_A^{-1}$ of the Cholesky factor $A = L_A L_A^\top$, which we cannot compute in the TT format, we proceed in two steps. We first solve the projected system of Eq. (22) with solver for the lower-triangular approximate inverse $\tilde{G}_L$, defined by $(\tilde{G}_L A)_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ for $i \ge j$ (Eq. (10)); this needs no Cholesky factor. We then set $G_L = D \tilde{G}_L$, truncated to totalmaxdim, where $D = [\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{G}_L)]^{-1/2}$ (Eq. (23)) is evaluated element-wise by RSI with uniform sketches (elementwise_function, Sec. 3.2). For an exact $\tilde{G}_L$ this gives $G_L = L_A^{-1}$ and $M^{-1} = A^{-1}$. The bond dimension of $G_L$ plays the role of the sparsity pattern of the classical FSAI: it sets the cost of storing and applying the preconditioner, at the expense of its quality.

The cost is dominated by the solve for $\tilde{G}_L$, an MPS with $N$ sites of dimension $d^2$ whose operator contains $A$ and the projector $\mathbb{P}_L$ of bond dimension 2 (Eq. (14)) twice. In the paper's experiments we use $\mu = 10^7$ (the results do not change for $\mu$ between $10^5$ and $10^8$, and the exact solution does not depend on $\mu$, Appendix B.1), build $\tilde{G}_L$ with MALS (ResidualALS) at a local tolerance between $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-8}$, stop once the residual of Eq. (22) is below $10^{-1}$ (residual_tol = 0.1), and keep $\chi_{G_L}$ between 4 and 10. The FAI pays off when it is reused, e.g. across the time steps of the Chorin projection, and when the local dimension is small (Sec. 5.2); with a large local dimension $d^2$ the build becomes expensive (Sec. 5.1). Note two limitations. The shortcut of Eq. (23) for $D$ holds only if $\tilde{G}_L$ solves Eq. (10) exactly; Lemma 1 bounds the error of each diagonal entry by the relative energy error of its row, but on strongly non-uniform grids or with strongly varying coefficients this shortcut can lose accuracy (Appendix B.6).

The value of $\mu$ sets the conditioning of the build, although not its exact solution. Since $\mathbb{P}_L$ acts on each row of $\tilde{G}_L$ separately, the operator of Eq. (22) is block diagonal, with the leading principal submatrices of $A$, whose eigenvalues lie in $[\lambda_{\min}(A), \lambda_{\max}(A)]$, and $\mu$ times the identity on the entries above the diagonal, so its condition number is $\max(\mu, \lambda_{\max}(A)) / \min(\mu, \lambda_{\min}(A))$. A $\mu$ far above $\lambda_{\max}(A)$, such as $10^7$ on a coarse grid, multiplies it by $\mu / \lambda_{\max}(A)$: the build converges more slowly and, with a capped number of sweeps, can return a worse preconditioner (on a $2^5 \times 2^5$ QTT pressure operator with $\lambda_{\max}(A) \approx 8 \cdot 10^3$, $\chi_{G_L} = 4$ and 30 MALS sweeps, $\mu = 10^7$ gave $\kappa(G_L^\top G_L A) = 146$ and $\mu = 10^3$ gave 5.3). Since $\lambda_{\max}(A)$ grows as $h^{-2}$, the paper's $\mu = 10^7$ is close to the top of the spectrum only on its finest grids. A value at the top of the spectrum, $\mu \approx \lambda_{\max}(A)$, is a reasonable choice: it keeps the condition number of the build at $\kappa(A)$ and weights the entries above the diagonal at least as strongly as any eigenvalue of $A$. A rough estimate suffices, e.g. from the stencil (at most $4/h^2$ per axis for the second-order Laplacian) or from a few Lanczos iterations.

DifferenTT.KrylovSpectrumStats — Type
KrylovSpectrumStats(; max_blockdim=4096)

Record of the sign-aware spectra of the local problems of a DMRG-type solve. Pass it as spectrum_store in LocalUpdaterArgs.

A condition number cannot tell whether a truncated approximate inverse has lost its definiteness (Fig. 3 of Torrente et al.), so we record the extreme eigenvalues with their sign. The local blocks are congruences $U^\top A U$ and $U^\top M^{-1} U$ of the global operators, where $U$ is the basis of the current projection. By Sylvester's law of inertia, a negative eigenvalue of the symmetric part of the local $M^{-1}$ therefore certifies that the global $M^{-1}$ is not positive definite; the converse does not hold, since a positive local block only shows that $M^{-1}$ is positive on the span of $U$.

We store one record (a NamedTuple) per local solve, with the fields

  • sweep, position, blockdim: sweep, first site and dimension $n_{\text{loc}}$ of the local problem;
  • numiter, converged, normres: the convergence information of the local Krylov solve;
  • eigmin_A, eigmax_A, asym_A: extreme eigenvalues of the symmetric part of $A_{\text{loc}}$, and its relative asymmetry $\|A - A^\top\| / \|A\|$;
  • eigmin_P, eigmax_P, nneg_P, asym_P: the same for $M^{-1}_{\text{loc}}$, with the number of negative eigenvalues beyond rounding;
  • eigmin_PA, eigmax_PA, nneg_PA: the spectrum of the PCG operator, when the symmetric part of $A_{\text{loc}}$ is positive definite.

The spectra cost $\mathcal{O}(n_{\text{loc}}^3)$. Blocks with $n_{\text{loc}} >$ max_blockdim are not diagonalized, and the fields that are not computed (all those of $M^{-1}$ for an unpreconditioned solve) are NaN, or -1 for the counts. spectrum_table returns the records column-wise.

DifferenTT.KrylovSweepStats — Type
KrylovSweepStats()

Record of the iteration counts of the local Krylov solves of a DMRG-type solve. Pass it as numiter_store in LocalUpdaterArgs.

The iteration counts of the local solver are what a preconditioner is meant to reduce, and they vary strongly along the tensor train (Fig. 11 of Torrente et al.), so we store them per position and per sweep. The layout of krylov_numiters is position → sweep → visits:

  • position is the first site of the local problem, from 1 to N - nsite + 1 for a tensor train of N sites;
  • sweep counts the sweeps from 1;
  • visits holds one count per local solve at that position and sweep, in the order of the solves. Every sweep visits every position twice, once in the forward half-sweep and once in the reverse one, so a full sweep stores two counts per position; the end positions are visited twice in a row, at the turn of the sweep.

We detect a new sweep when the first position is solved again after the solve has moved away from it, since every sweep starts there. The same object can therefore be reused across solves, whose sweeps are numbered on, but a tensor train with a single position (N = nsite) records all its solves under sweep 1. AMEn with local_stop may stop after a forward half-sweep, which leaves one count per position in its last sweep.

See also positions_mean, positions_sum, KrylovCondStats.

DifferenTT.KrylovUpdater — Type
KrylovUpdater() <: AbstractUpdater

Local solver that solves each local problem by a Krylov method, the default of every DMRG-type solver.

We pass the local problem to KrylovKit.linsolve with the fields of LocalUpdaterArgs, so that KrylovKit selects CG when ishermitian && isposdef and restarted GMRES otherwise. The local operator is never formed: each iteration contracts it with the current local tensor, at a cost of $\mathcal{O}(\chi^3 \chi_A d^2 + \chi^2 \chi_A^2 d^3)$ for a 2-site problem, where $\chi$ and $\chi_A$ are the bond dimensions of the solution and of A. A preconditioned local problem is solved instead by preconditioned_linsolve, which runs PCG or left-preconditioned GMRES with the projected preconditioner (Sec. 4 of Torrente et al.).

The local diagnostics of LocalUpdaterArgs (numiter_store, cond_store, pc_cond_store, spectrum_store, spd_tol) are recorded or checked after each local solve.

Solve the local problem $(a_0 + a_1 A_{\text{loc}}) \mathbf{x}_{\text{loc}} = \mathbf{b}_{\text{loc}}$, with coefficients = (a₀, a₁), from the initial guess init. The kwargs are the fields of LocalUpdaterArgs.

The first method calls KrylovKit.linsolve (CG if ishermitian && isposdef, GMRES otherwise). The second one calls preconditioned_linsolve with the projected preconditioner of problem, so it runs PCG or left-preconditioned GMRES, except at the local problems whose first site (lpos + 1) is not in preconditioner_sites, which are solved unpreconditioned. After the solve, we record the iteration count in numiter_store, the spectra in spectrum_store, and, only if cond_store, pc_cond_store or spd_tol is set, the condition numbers $\kappa = \max|\lambda| / \min|\lambda|$ of the dense local $A_{\text{loc}}$ and $M^{-1}_{\text{loc}} A_{\text{loc}}$. This is the spectral condition number that bounds the convergence of CG and PCG; for GMRES it is only a heuristic indicator, since it is a lower bound on $\kappa_2$ for non-normal operators and GMRES also depends on the eigenvector conditioning. With spd_tol set and CG selected, a local operator with an eigenvalue whose real part is below -spd_tol * max|λ| throws a NotSPDError. With verbosity > 0, the computed condition numbers are logged.

DifferenTT.NotSPDError — Type

CG requires an SPD operator, and a truncated approximate inverse can lose its definiteness (Fig. 3 of Torrente et al.), in which case PCG may stagnate or diverge without any error. preconditioned_linsolve(f, b, x₀, M⁻¹, a₀::Number=0, a₁::Number=1; alg_rrule=nothing, kwargs...) -> (x, info)

Solve $(a_0 + a_1 f)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ from x₀ with the preconditioner M⁻¹, the preconditioned counterpart of KrylovKit.linsolve(f, b, x₀, a₀, a₁; kwargs...).

This is a function of its own rather than a method of KrylovKit.linsolve, because DifferenTT owns none of the argument types and such a method would be type piracy.

Returns the solution and a KrylovKit.ConvergenceInfo.

DifferenTT.GlobalCG — Type
GlobalCG(; updater_kwargs=GlobalUpdaterArgs(isposdef=true, ishermitian=true)) <: AbstractGlobalSolver
GlobalCG(updater_kwargs::GlobalUpdaterArgs)

Global CG solver for an SPD $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ in the TT format: linearsolve runs a TTCG built from updater_kwargs on the whole tensor train.

A global solver needs no sweeps and no local problems, but every iteration increases the bond dimensions of its iterates, which we have to truncate, so its cost and its convergence both depend on how compressible the Krylov vectors are (see AbstractGlobalSolver). The global solvers are not used in the experiments of Torrente et al. With a preconditioner, M⁻¹ is applied to the whole residual rather than to local problems.

Fields

Throws

x = linearsolve(solver, A, b, nothing)

DifferenTT.TTCG — Type
TTCG(; maxiter=100, tol=1e-8, verbosity=0, maxdim=typemax(Int), cutoff=1e-8, orth_maxdim=typemax(Int),
     orth_cutoff=1e-12) <: KrylovKit.KrylovAlgorithm

Conjugate gradient (CG) algorithm on tensor trains, run by KrylovKit.linsolve(A, b, x₀, [M⁻¹,] alg::TTCG, a₀, a₁) and by GlobalCG.

Its iterates are MPS whose bond dimensions are truncated after each operation, so that the cost stays bounded. We therefore evaluate each step length with the exact sandwich $\langle \mathbf{p}, A \mathbf{p} \rangle$ rather than with the truncated product $A\mathbf{p}$, and recompute the residual from $\mathbf{x}$ when the recurrence reports convergence, restarting CG if the true residual is still above the tolerance. Note that truncation still perturbs the conjugacy of the search directions, so the convergence of CG is not guaranteed when the truncation is severe (Sec. 2.1 of Torrente et al.).

Fields

  • maxiter::Int=100: maximum number of iterations.
  • tol::Float64=1e-8: tolerance relative to $\|\mathbf{b}\|$: the solve stops at $\|\mathbf{r}\| \le \texttt{tol}\, \|\mathbf{b}\|$.
  • verbosity::Int=0: KrylovKit verbosity (1: warn on no convergence, 2: start and end, 3: every iteration).
  • maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), cutoff::Float64=1e-8: truncation of the applications of A and M⁻¹ and of the updates of $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{r}$.
  • orth_maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), orth_cutoff::Float64=1e-12: truncation of the new search direction $\mathbf{p}$.

DifferenTT.TTGMRES — Type
TTGMRES(; krylovdim=30, maxiter=100, tol=1e-8, verbosity=0, maxdim=typemax(Int), cutoff=1e-8,
        orth_maxdim=typemax(Int), orth_cutoff=1e-12) <: KrylovKit.KrylovAlgorithm

Restarted GMRES algorithm on tensor trains (Dolgov, 2013), run by KrylovKit.linsolve(A, b, x₀, [M⁻¹,] alg::TTGMRES, a₀, a₁) and by GlobalGMRES.

Each cycle builds an Arnoldi basis of at most krylovdim MPS by classical Gram–Schmidt: every entry $H_{ik} = \langle \mathbf{v}_i, A \mathbf{v}_k \rangle$ of the Hessenberg matrix is an exact sandwich with the untruncated $\mathbf{v}_k$, while the orthogonalized vector is truncated with orth_maxdim and orth_cutoff. Since the basis is truncated and never reorthogonalized, it is only approximately orthonormal, and the least-squares residual of the cycle is only an estimate. We therefore recompute the residual from $\mathbf{x}$ at the end of every cycle, at the cost of one more application of A, and stop only when it is below the tolerance.

Fields

  • krylovdim::Int=30: maximum dimension of the Krylov basis before a restart.
  • maxiter::Int=100: maximum number of restart cycles; info.numiter counts the cycles.
  • tol::Float64=1e-8: tolerance relative to $\|\mathbf{b}\|$.
  • verbosity::Int=0: KrylovKit verbosity (1: warn on no convergence, 2: start and end, 3: every step).
  • maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), cutoff::Float64=1e-8: truncation of the applications of A and M⁻¹ and of the updates of $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{r}$.
  • orth_maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), orth_cutoff::Float64=1e-12: truncation of the Gram–Schmidt step.
