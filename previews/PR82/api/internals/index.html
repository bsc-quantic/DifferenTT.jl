Internals

The names below are neither exported nor declared public. They are not part of the API covered by semantic versioning and may change in any release; we list them because the docstrings of the public API link to them, and because they document how the library is built. Each section collects the internal names of one area of the API reference, grouped by source file.

Internal names: tensor trains

DifferenTT.Architecture — Type
Architecture

Abstract supertype of the layouts that place the variables of an $N$-dimensional grid on the sites of a tensor train: the quantized QTTArchitecture, with one bit of one variable per site, and the TTArchitecture, with one variable per site. Every subtype has the fields var_map and site_map, the latter giving the variable carried by each site, and implements nsites_per_variable, total_sites, site_dim, site_dims and variable_dims.

DifferenTT._rsi_tt_mul — Method
_rsi_tt_mul(mps1::MPS, mps2::MPS; nsite=2, oversample=25, target_rank::Int=0, pivot_rtol::Real=1e-10,
            outputlevel=0, rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng(), kws...) -> MPS

Return the element-wise (Hadamard) product of two MPS by recursive sketched interpolation (RSI): at every bond, from left to right, we sketch the suffixes of both factors (suffix_sketches), multiply the two sketched blocks of nsite sites element-wise, and take the interpolative decomposition (interpolative) of the result, whose pivots give the next core of the product; both factors are then re-expressed on the same pivots before moving on (Fig. 5 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format). The output is not orthogonalized, and it can throw, so callers need a fallback (tt_mul). With fewer than nsite sites the product of the full tensors is returned instead.

The cross is deliberately run without a rank cap. Capping it truncates on greedily chosen interpolation pivots instead of on singular values, which costs two to three orders of magnitude in accuracy at a given rank, so tt_mul caps the result once, by SVD, afterwards. The raw rank stays bounded by the sketch size of max(χ₁, χ₂, target_rank) + oversample columns. This bound is also the main limitation: the product of two MPS can need a rank up to $\chi_1 \chi_2$, and when it exceeds the sketch size the result is not merely truncated but can be far off, and nothing reports it (#55). For two random MPS of bond dimension 16 on 16 sites, whose product has rank 256, the relative error was 45 with the default sketch, 5 with target_rank = 64, and at round-off level from target_rank = 128. On the low-rank products of smooth fields it agrees with the direct product to the accuracy of the inputs.

Keywords

  • nsite=2: number of sites decomposed at a time.
  • oversample=25: sketch columns on top of the largest rank. This is the accuracy knob of the uncapped cross: the sketch spans the face-splitting product of the two factors, which is larger than the range of their element-wise product, so the sketch size sets an error floor (about 1e-4 at oversample = 5, 1e-5 at 15 and 1e-6 at 40, for 30 to 100 % more runtime).
  • target_rank::Int=0: expected rank of the product, which can exceed both input ranks. Pass the rank that the same product had at the previous step when it is known.
  • pivot_rtol::Real=1e-10: pivot threshold of the cross, relative to the block being decomposed (pivot_cutoff).
  • outputlevel=0: print the ranks of the product if at least 1.
  • rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng(): random number generator of the Gaussian sketches.

Other keywords are ignored.

The recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm is that of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire, arXiv:2602.17974. Our implementation started from a rough implementation that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.

DifferenTT.interpolative — Method
interpolative(T::ITensor, col_inds, local_vert; pivot_index::Union{Nothing,Index}=nothing, tags="Link", kws...)
    -> (C, Z, error)

Return the interpolative decomposition $T \approx C Z$ of the ITensor T, seen as a matrix whose columns are the indices col_inds and whose rows are the others. $C$ holds the pivot columns of T, with the row indices of T, and $Z$ interpolates them, with the indices col_inds; the two share a new PivotIndex that records, for every pivot, the values of col_inds that select it, with the values of the vertex local_vert written as local_vert => value and pivot indices among col_inds expanded into their own pivots. This is how the crosses of RSI keep track of the multi-indices behind each bond. The pivot columns are chosen by the prrldu algorithm, with kws (cutoff, maxdim, mindim); if pivot_index is given, its pivots fix the columns instead, which are then fitted by the "nuclear" algorithm, and the pivots are checked to be those of pivot_index. The decomposition runs on the CPU, and C and Z are moved back to the array backend of T.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if the pivots of pivot_index do not match the columns they select.

This routine came with the implementation of the recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire (arXiv:2602.17974) that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.

source
DifferenTT.interpolative — Method
interpolative(M::Matrix; algorithm="prrldu", kws...) -> (C, Z, piv_cols, error)

Return the interpolative decomposition $M \approx C Z$ of the matrix M, where $C$ consists of columns of M (those listed in piv_cols) and $Z$ is an interpolating matrix, equal to the identity on those columns. The rank is set by cutoff, maxdim and mindim. An interpolative decomposition keeps actual entries of M in its factor $C$, which is what lets RSI rebuild a tensor train from sketched entries (Sec. 3.2 and Fig. 5 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format).

Keywords

  • algorithm="prrldu": "prrldu" (pivoted LDU, prrldu) or "nuclear" (greedy Gram-matrix selection, needed when cols is given).
  • cols=nothing: columns to use for $C$; then the rank parameters are ignored.
  • cutoff, maxdim, mindim: rank selection; see the method of each algorithm.

This routine came with the implementation of the recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire (arXiv:2602.17974) that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.

DifferenTT.interpolative — Method
interpolative(::Algorithm"nuclear", M::Matrix; cols=nothing, cutoff=0.0, maxdim=min(size(M)...), kws...)
    -> (C, Z, cols, error)

Return the interpolative decomposition $M \approx C Z$ with $C = M_{:, \text{cols}}$. If cols is nothing, the columns are chosen greedily on the Gram matrix $K = M^\top M$: at every step we add the column that most reduces the residual $K_t = K - K_{:,c} K_{c,c}^{-1} K_{c,:}$, measured by $(K_t^2)_{pp} / (K_t)_{pp}$, until the square root of that measure, returned as error, falls below cutoff or maxdim columns are taken. $Z$ is the least-squares solution of $C Z = M$, by C \ M, or by the pseudo-inverse if use_pinv = true is passed in kws, and its pivot columns are then set to the identity exactly. With prescribed cols, error is Inf.

This routine came with the implementation of the recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire (arXiv:2602.17974) that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.

source
DifferenTT.interpolative — Method
interpolative(::Algorithm"prrldu", M::Matrix; cols=nothing, kws...) -> (C, Z, piv_cols, inf_error)

Return the interpolative decomposition $M \approx C Z$ computed from the prrldu of M, to which kws (cutoff, maxdim, mindim) are passed. $C$ holds the pivot columns M[:, piv_cols] and $Z = U_{11}^{-1} U$, where $U_{11}$ is the leading square block of the unit upper factor, so that Z[:, piv_cols] is the identity. DifferenTT.pivot_cutoff — Method
pivot_cutoff(T::ITensor, rtol::Real) -> Float64

Return the pivot threshold max(rtol * maximum(abs, T), floatmin(Float64)) for the interpolative decomposition of T, relative to its largest entry. prrldu compares pivots with an absolute threshold and divides by every pivot it keeps, so an absolute cutoff behaves differently on operands of different magnitude (p*dt, RK4 increments, a zero velocity component): it either discards every pivot, and the cross then throws, or it keeps, and inverts, round-off pivots. The floatmin floor keeps the threshold strictly positive, so that the pivot search of an all-zero block stops instead of dividing 0 by 0.

DifferenTT.prrldu — Method
prrldu(M::Matrix; cutoff::Real=0.0, maxdim::Int=typemax(Int), mindim::Int=1) -> (L, d, U, pr, pc, inf_error)

Return the pivoted, rank-revealing LDU decomposition $M \approx L D U$ of any matrix M, rectangular or rank deficient, with L[pr, :] * Diagonal(d) * U[:, pc] ≈ M.

The pivots are chosen by complete pivoting: at every step we take the entry of largest magnitude of the current Schur complement, and stop when it falls below cutoff or after $\min(n_r, n_c)$ steps. The approximation error is then the Schur complement that is left, whose largest entry is returned as inf_error (0 if the search did not stop on cutoff). We then build the factors along this pivot order, keeping at most maxdim pivots and at least mindim, even if they are zero, and stopping at the first pivot below cutoff after those. Note that cutoff is an absolute threshold on the entries, not a relative one, and that inf_error does not account for pivots dropped by maxdim.

Returns

  • L: $n_r \times r$ unit lower triangular factor, in pivoted row order.
  • d: the $r$ pivots.
  • U: $r \times n_c$ unit upper triangular factor, in pivoted column order.
  • pr, pc: the permutations that undo the pivoting of the rows and columns.
  • inf_error: the largest entry of the Schur complement where the pivot search stopped.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if maxdim or mindim is not positive.

This routine came with the implementation of the recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire (arXiv:2602.17974) that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.

source
DifferenTT.sketch — Method
sketch(M::AbstractMPS, region_verts, k::Index; rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng()) -> ITensor
positive_sketch(M::AbstractMPS, region_verts, k::Index; rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng()) -> ITensor

Return the random sketch of the sites region_verts of M: for each of the dim(k) values of the sketch index k, we contract every site of the region with an independent random vector, Gaussian for sketch and uniform in $[0, 1)$ for positive_sketch, so that the sketch of an entrywise-positive tensor train stays positive. The result keeps the link indices at the ends of the region and k; an empty region gives the scalar 1. The random vectors are drawn on the CPU from rng, so that a seed gives the same sketch on every backend, and moved to the backend of M. Each region costs $\mathcal{O}(|\text{region}| \, k \, d \chi^2)$. The crosses use suffix_sketches instead, which builds the sketches of all suffixes in one pass; these single-region versions stay as the references the tests compare it with.

source
DifferenTT.suffix_sketches — Method
suffix_sketches(M::AbstractMPS, k::Index; positive::Bool=false, rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng())
    -> Vector{ITensor}

Return the random sketches of every suffix of M, accumulated in a single right-to-left pass: S[v] is the sketch of the sites v:length(M), distributed as sketch(M, v:length(M), k) builds it, or as positive_sketch with positive = true (uniform, positive sketch vectors instead of Gaussian ones), and S[length(M)+1] is the empty, scalar sketch. Building every suffix on its own would cost $\mathcal{O}(N^2 k d \chi^2)$; extending one running contraction per sketch column costs $\mathcal{O}(N k d \chi^2)$. Note that the sketches of different suffixes share their random vectors, which a sketch built per suffix would not. Both crosses, of _rsi_tt_mul and of elementwise_function, use it: they only rewrite tensors at or to the left of their current region, so the suffixes they need are always those of the untouched input.

TensorCrossInterpolation.crossinterpolate2 — Method
TensorCrossInterpolation.crossinterpolate2(func_type::Type{<:Number}, func::Function, grid::Grid{N},
    arch::Architecture; initialpivots=[ones(Int, total_sites(arch))], tolerance::Float64=1e-8,
    maxbonddim::Int=100, maxiter::Int=50, verbosity::Int=0, kwargs...)

Build a tensor train of the function func of the coordinates, sampled on the nodes of grid and laid out by arch, by the two-site tensor cross interpolation of TensorCrossInterpolation.jl (Núñez Fernández et al., 2025).

Cross interpolation needs only the values of func at the entries it selects, and not the full tensor, so it builds the TT format of a function on a grid far too large to be stored densely, provided that the function has a low-rank representation in that layout. Every multi-index requested by TCI (1-based, one value per site) is mapped to the coordinates of its node by sequence_to_point, and func(point::NTuple{N,Float64}) is converted to func_type; the requests are evaluated in batches on all threads. Since TCI starts from initialpivots, a function that vanishes on the default pivot (the node at the origin) needs another one, e.g. from optfirstpivot.

Keywords

  • initialpivots::Vector{Vector{Int64}}: 1-based multi-indices of the initial pivots, total_sites(arch) entries each.
  • tolerance::Float64=1e-8, maxbonddim::Int=100, maxiter::Int=50, verbosity::Int=0, and kwargs...: passed to TensorCrossInterpolation.crossinterpolate2.

Returns

  • The output of TensorCrossInterpolation.crossinterpolate2: the TensorCI2 object, the ranks and the errors of every iteration. ITensorMPS.MPS(tci) turns it into an MPS, whose site indices are then replaced by those of the problem with ITensorMPS.replace_siteinds!.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if initialpivots is empty.
  • DimensionMismatch: if a pivot does not have one entry per site.

DifferenTT.neumann_correction_matrix — Method
neumann_correction_matrix(derivative_order::Int, side::Symbol; stencil_order::Int=8) -> Matrix{Float64}

Return the $q \times q$ corner block $M$ that the homogeneous Neumann closure subtracts from a one-dimensional operator of derivative order derivative_order and stencil order $q$ = stencil_order (2 or 8).

The boundary lies on the stored node $u_0$ and the ghost values are mirrored, $u_{-k} = u_k$. The coefficient $c_k$ that row $i$ applies to a ghost node $u_{i+k}$, $i + k < 0$, therefore moves to the stored node $u_{-(i+k)}$. With 0-based row and column indices of the block, side = :left gives

\[M_{i,m} = -c_{-(i+m)}, \qquad 0 \le i < q/2, \quad 1 \le m \le q/2 - i,\]

with every other entry zero, for the top-left corner, and side = :right gives the reflected block $M_{q-1-i,\,q-1-m} = -c_{i+m}$ for the bottom-right corner. The closed operator is $D - M$, where $D$ is the truncated Toeplitz operator and $c_k$ are the coefficients of derivative_coefficients. Any other side returns the zero block.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if stencil_order is not 2 or 8.

Examples

julia> DifferenTT.neumann_correction_matrix(2, :left; stencil_order=2)
2×2 Matrix{Float64}:

  0.0   0.0

Build the unscaled ($h = 1$) one-dimensional operator $C_L$ of axis axis whose product with the all-ones vector gives, on each row, the stencil coefficients that the truncation at the left side dropped.

Return the two factors of the FAI of the SPD operator $A$: the lower-triangular approximate inverse $\tilde{G}_L$ and the diagonal rescaling $D$, which fai_preconditioner multiplies into $G_L = D \tilde{G}_L$. We keep the two factors apart so that the effects of truncating $\tilde{G}_L$ and $D$ can be studied separately when prototyping; call it qualified, DifferenTT.fai_dg.

We vectorize $X = \tilde{G}_L$ by fusing, at each site, its row index (a copy of the sites, the output of $\tilde{G}_L$) with its column index into one index of dimension $d^2$, and solve with g_solver the projected system of Eq. (22) of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format,

\[\left[\mathbb{P}_L \mathcal{T}_A \mathbb{P}_L + \mu (\mathbb{I} - \mathbb{P}_L)\right] \mathrm{vec}(\tilde{G}_L) = \mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{I}),\]

where $\mathcal{T}_A \mathrm{vec}(X) = \mathrm{vec}(XA)$ is the operator written $\mathbf{I} \otimes A$ in Eq. (19) for a symmetric $A$, $\mathbb{P}_L$ = triang_projector_mpo keeps the lower-triangular entries of $X$ (Eqs. (14)–(20)), and $\mu > 0$ weights the equations $\mu X_{ij} = 0$ of the entries above the diagonal. Its exact solution satisfies Eq. (10), $(\tilde{G}_L A)_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ for $i \ge j$, and does not depend on $\mu$ (Appendix B.1). The initial guess is $10^{-3}$ times the lower-triangular matrix of ones. We then extract $\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{G}_L)$ as an MPS with delta tensors (diagmps), and evaluate $D = [\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{G}_L)]^{-1/2}$ (Eq. (23)) element-wise by RSI with uniform sketches (elementwise_function, Sec. 3.2). The inverse square root is evaluated as $1/\sqrt{\max(x, \epsilon)}$, so a non-positive entry, which Lemma 1 rules out unless its whole row is lost, gives a large finite entry of $D$ rather than an error.

D_kwargs may hold the cutoff (default 1e-15), maxdim (default typemax(Int)) and oversample (default 10) of elementwise_function. With preconditioning = true, the system is preconditioned by $\mathbb{P}_L R \mathbb{P}_L + \mu^{-1}(\mathbb{I} - \mathbb{P}_L)$, where $R$ is the rank1_preconditioner of its operator and $\mu^{-1}$ inverts the complement block. Note that this does not speed the build up: on a $2^6$-point QTT Laplacian with ResidualALS, it multiplied the local CG iterations by 3.1 for $\mu = 1$, 1.04 for $\mu = 10^3$ and 7.8 for $\mu = 10^7$, for a preconditioner of the same quality; residual_tol adds a ResidualObserver on the system as sweep_observer!; compute_residual = true logs the relative residual of the system after the solve, at the cost of two more sandwiches with its operator; the other kwargs go to linearsolve.

DifferenTT._residual_rank — Method
_residual_rank(reduced_operator, phi, rhs, U_full, S_full, V_full; resid_tol, resid_damp,
               frob_weight = 0.0, mindim = 1, maxdim = typemax(Int))

Return the smallest rank r such that truncating the SVD phi = U_full * S_full * V_full to its r largest singular values keeps the relative local residual ‖A_loc φ_r - rhs‖ / ‖rhs‖ below max(resid_tol, res_exact) * resid_damp, where res_exact is the relative local residual of phi. The reduced operator must be positioned at the site(s) of phi, and rhs must be its local right-hand side (_local_rhs), with norm(rhs) > 0.

On top of that, a Frobenius floor keeps every rank whose discarded weight Σ_{k>r} σ_k² / Σ σ_k² would exceed frob_weight (0.0 disables it). The result is clamped to [mindim, maxdim].

Returns

  • (chosen_rank, truncerr, res_exact), with truncerr = sqrt(discarded weight / total weight).
