Tensor trains

Architectures, constructors, operations, element-wise products, and cross interpolation in the TT and QTT formats. The manual pages describe the conventions.

Exported names and names declared public are listed here; the other names of the area are on the Internals page.

DifferenTT.QTTArchitecture — Type
QTTArchitecture{N} <: Architecture

Layout of an $N$-dimensional grid of $2^{n_1} \times \dots \times 2^{n_N}$ points in the QTT format, where every site carries one bit of the index of one variable, so that all sites have dimension $d = 2$.

A variable with $2^n$ grid points is quantized by the binary expansion $i = \sum_{p=1}^{n} i_p 2^{n-p}$ of its index (Sec. 2 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format), and therefore takes $n$ sites. The architecture decides where these sites go along the chain. In the blocked layout the sites of each variable are contiguous, and the blocks follow the positions given by var_map. In the interleaved layout we take one site of each variable in turn, in the same order, and skip the variables that have run out of sites. Within a variable, the bits run from the most significant to the least significant along the chain, unless its entry of var_nsites is negative, in which case its first site carries the least significant bit.

Fields

  • var_nsites::NTuple{N,Int}: number of sites (bits) of each variable; a negative entry reverses its bit order.
  • var_map::Dict{Int,Int}: variable => position of each variable, among the blocks (blocked) or within every cycle (interleaved).
  • site_map::Vector{Int}: variable carried by each site.
  • is_interleaved::Bool: whether the layout is interleaved.

See also TTArchitecture, site_indices.

QTTArchitecture(var_nsites::NTuple{N,Int}, var_map::Union{Dict{Int,Int},Nothing}=nothing; interleaved::Bool=false)

Build the QTT layout of N variables with abs(var_nsites[i]) sites each, and compute its site_map.

DifferenTT.TTArchitecture — Type
TTArchitecture{N} <: Architecture
TTArchitecture(var_dims::NTuple{N,Int}, var_map::Union{Dict{Int,Int},Nothing}=nothing)

Layout of an $N$-dimensional grid of $n_1 \times \dots \times n_N$ points in the TT format, with one site per variable. The site that carries variable $i$ has dimension $n_i$, so the sites have different dimensions whenever the variables do. The constructor places variable $i$ on site var_map[i], or on site $i$ if var_map is nothing, and throws an ArgumentError if var_map is not a permutation of 1:N.

Fields

  • var_dims::NTuple{N,Int}: number of grid points of each variable.
  • var_map::Dict{Int,Int}: variable => site of each variable.
  • site_map::Vector{Int}: variable carried by each site, the inverse permutation of var_map.

Examples

julia> arch = TTArchitecture((5, 7, 3), Dict(1 => 3, 2 => 1, 3 => 2));

julia> Tuple(arch.site_map), site_dims(arch), variable_dims(arch)
((2, 3, 1), (7, 3, 5), (5, 7, 3))

See also QTTArchitecture.

DifferenTT.triang_projector_mpo — Function
triang_projector_mpo(sites::Vector{<:Index}, combiners::Vector{ITensor}, which_triang::Symbol=:upper,
                     include_diag::Bool=true; array_type=Array) -> MPO
triang_projector_mpo(sites::Vector{<:Index}, which_triang::Symbol=:upper, include_diag::Bool=true;
                     array_type=Array) -> MPO

Return the projector $\mathcal{P}_L$ that keeps the lower (or upper) triangular entries of a vectorized matrix and sets the others to zero, as an MPO of bond dimension 2.

In the FAI construction the unknown is a lower triangular matrix $\tilde{G}_L$, which we vectorize in order to solve for it with the linear solvers of the package. Its triangularity then becomes a constraint on the vector, which we impose by projection (Sec. 3.1 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format). To this end, we take the mask of lower_triang_mpo, vectorize it, and place it on the diagonal of an operator in the vectorized space, by contracting each site with a delta tensor (Sec. 3.1 and Fig. 4). The projector therefore keeps the bond dimension 2 of the mask, and its diagonal entries are 1 on the kept entries and 0 on the others.

The matrix $X$ on sites (rows on sites', columns on sites) is vectorized site by site: site $k$ of the vector carries the pair (column, row) of site $k$ of $X$, merged by combiners[k]. Every combiner must merge two indices of dimension dim(sites[k]), the first standing for the column and the second for the row; only their dimensions and their order matter. For example, fai_dg vectorizes $\tilde{G}_L$ with combiner(sites[k], helper_sites[k]), where the helper sites carry the row index and become the output sites sites' of $\tilde{G}_L$, so that the projector with which_triang = :lower keeps the entries with row $\ge$ column, in the order of contract_mpo (site 1 most significant). On a single site of dimension 2, its diagonal is $(1, 0, 1, 1)$, as in the example of Sec. 3.1. The second method builds the combiners combiner(sites[k], sites[k]') itself.

Arguments

  • sites::Vector{<:Index}: sites of the matrix being vectorized.
  • combiners::Vector{ITensor}: one combiner per site, merging (column, row).
  • which_triang::Symbol=:upper: :lower or :upper.
  • include_diag::Bool=true: keep the diagonal entries.

Keywords

  • array_type::Type{<:AbstractArray}=Array: array backend of the tensors.

Returns

  • MPO: the projector, on the combined indices combinedind.(combiners) and their primes.

Throws

  • DimensionMismatch: if sites and combiners have different lengths.
  • ArgumentError: if which_triang is neither :lower nor :upper.

See also lower_triang_mpo, fai_preconditioner.

source
DifferenTT.upper_triang_mpo — Function
upper_triang_mpo(site_inds::Vector{<:Index}, include_diag::Bool=true; array_type=Array) -> MPO
lower_triang_mpo(sites::Vector{<:Index}, include_diag::Bool=true; array_type=Array) -> MPO

Return the upper (lower) triangular matrix of ones, with or without its diagonal, as an MPO of bond dimension 2.

The lower triangular matrix of ones is the mask $P_L^{(1:N)}$ of the FAI construction, whose entry $(i, j)$ is 1 if $i \ge j$ and 0 otherwise, in the order of contract_mpo (site 1 most significant). In order to build it without forming the dense matrix, we use its exact TT representation, Eq. (14) of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format:

\[P_L^{(1:N)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbb{I}^{(d_1)} & \mathbb{J}^{(d_1)} \end{bmatrix} \bowtie_{k=2}^{N-1} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbb{I}^{(d_k)} & \mathbb{J}^{(d_k)} \\ & \mathbb{O}^{(d_k)} \end{bmatrix} \bowtie \begin{bmatrix} \mathbb{L}^{(d_N)} \\ \mathbb{O}^{(d_N)} \end{bmatrix},\]

where $\bowtie$ is the strong Kronecker product, $\mathbb{I}$ the identity, $\mathbb{O}$ the matrix of ones, and $\mathbb{L}$ and $\mathbb{J}$ the lower triangular matrices of ones with and without the diagonal; without the diagonal, $\mathbb{J}$ replaces $\mathbb{L}$ in the last core. The decomposition holds for any site dimensions $d_k$, so it serves the TT and the QTT formats alike. upper_triang_mpo builds the transposed cores, and lower_triang_mpo is transpose_mpo of it.

Arguments

  • site_inds::Vector{<:Index}: sites of the operator, at least one.
  • include_diag::Bool=true: keep the diagonal.

Keywords

  • array_type::Type{<:AbstractArray}=Array: array backend of the tensors.

Examples

julia> sites = DifferenTT.site_indices([2, 3]);

julia> contract_mpo(DifferenTT.lower_triang_mpo(sites)) == [Float64(i >= j) for i in 1:6, j in 1:6]
true

See also triang_projector_mpo, which turns the mask into a projector in the vectorized space.

DifferenTT.contract_mpo — Method
contract_mpo(mpo::MPO) -> Matrix

Return the dense matrix of mpo, with rows indexed by the output sites (prime level 1) and columns by the input sites (prime level 0). Site 1 is the most significant index of both, so that the MPO of $A_1 \otimes A_2 \otimes \cdots$, with $A_k$ on site $k$, contracts to kron(A₁, A₂, …); this is the convention of every dense reference in the package. The element type is that of the tensors. It forms the full $\prod_k d_k \times \prod_k d_k$ matrix, so it is meant for tests and small examples.

Examples

s = siteinds(2, 2)
A, B = rand(2, 2), rand(2, 2)
M = MPO([ITensor(A, s[1]', s[1]), ITensor(B, s[2]', s[2])])  # bond dimension 1, without link indices
contract_mpo(M) ≈ kron(A, B)  # true

See also contract_mps, mat2mpo.

DifferenTT.ndim_operator_sum_mpo — Method
ndim_operator_sum_mpo(onedim_ops::NTuple{N,AbstractMatrix}, site_inds::Vector{<:Index}, arch::TTArchitecture{N};
                      pair_ops=nothing) -> MPO

Return the Kronecker sum $\sum_{c=1}^{N} \mathbb{I} \otimes \cdots \otimes A_c \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathbb{I}$ of the one-dimensional operators onedim_ops, one per variable, as an exact MPO of bond dimension 2.

Summing the $N$ lifted operators of ndim_operator_mpo with + would give bond dimension $N$ before rounding. We instead write the sum directly with the bond-dimension-2 cores of Kazeev & Khoromskij (2012), which behave as a two-state automaton along the chain: state 1 means that no operator has been placed yet and state 2 that one has, so the middle cores are $\begin{bmatrix} \mathbb{I} & A_{c(k)} \\ & \mathbb{I} \end{bmatrix}$, where $c$ = arch.site_map. In this way kronecker_laplacian keeps bond dimension 2 in any number of dimensions, and the pairwise term gives the mixed derivatives of equicorrelated_laplacian with bond dimension 3.

If pair_ops = (P, Q) is given, we also add the pairwise term

\[\sum_{i < j} P_{c(i)} \otimes Q_{c(j)},\]

where $i < j$ runs over the sites, with the identity on every other site. A third state ("$P$ placed, waiting for $Q$") is needed for it, so the bond dimension becomes 3. P and Q are indexed by variable, like onedim_ops, so the P of the variable on the last site and the Q of the variable on the first site are never used. On a single site the result is onedim_ops[1].

Arguments

  • onedim_ops::NTuple{N,AbstractMatrix}: one operator per variable, $n_c \times n_c$.
  • site_inds::Vector{<:Index}: one site index per variable, in site order (e.g. siteinds(arch)).
  • arch::TTArchitecture{N}: the layout.

Keywords

  • pair_ops::Union{Nothing,Tuple{NTuple{N,AbstractMatrix},NTuple{N,AbstractMatrix}}}=nothing: the operators (P, Q) of the pairwise term.

Throws

  • DimensionMismatch: if site_inds does not have N indices.

See also ndim_operator_mpo, and the method for a QTTArchitecture.

DifferenTT.toeplitz_mpo — Function
toeplitz_mpo(site_indices::Vector{<:Index}, coeffs::Dict{Int,Float64}, boundary_conditions::BoundaryCond1d,
             array_type::Type{<:AbstractArray}=Array) -> MPO
toeplitz_mpo(nsite::Int, coeffs::Dict{Int,Float64}, boundary_conditions::BoundaryCond1d,
             array_type::Type{<:AbstractArray}=Array) -> MPO

Return the banded Toeplitz matrix $A_{i, i+k} = c_k$ of a finite-difference stencil on a one-dimensional QTT grid of $2^N$ points, as an MPO of bond dimension 3, or 9 on its last bond for the wider stencil.

A stencil applied on a uniform grid is a sum of shifted identities, and in the QTT format every shift by a fixed offset has an exact representation of bond dimension 2, since adding the offset to a binary index only carries one bit from each site to the next (Kazeev & Khoromskij, 2012). We build the whole band at once: every core is written in terms of the single-site shift $J = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, its transpose and the identity, the bond carries the carry of the binary addition, and the coefficients enter only in the last core (site $N$, the least significant bit). With coeffs at the offsets $-1, 0, 1$ (a second-order stencil) every bond has dimension 3; with the offsets $-4, \dots, 4$ (an eighth-order stencil, $q = 8$) the last two cores also resolve the offsets up to 4, and the last bond has dimension 9. If boundary_conditions.left is a PeriodicBC, the band wraps around and the result is the circulant matrix, $A_{i, j} = c_k$ whenever $j - i \equiv k \pmod{2^N}$; otherwise the entries that would fall outside the matrix are dropped, and the boundary rows are left to the corrections of the boundary conditions. Only the type of boundary_conditions.left is read.

Arguments

  • site_indices::Vector{<:Index}: sites of dimension 2, site 1 the most significant bit; the second method creates nsite new ones.
  • coeffs::Dict{Int,Float64}: the coefficient $c_k$ of each offset $k$, with keys -1:1 or -4:4.
  • boundary_conditions::BoundaryCond1d: periodic or not, read from its left condition.
  • array_type::Type{<:AbstractArray}=Array: array backend of the tensors.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if coeffs does not have 3 or 9 entries, or if there are fewer than 2 sites (3 coefficients) or 4 sites (9 coefficients).
  • KeyError: if an offset of the expected range is missing from coeffs.

Examples

bc = BoundaryCond1d(PeriodicBC(), PeriodicBC())
A = DifferenTT.toeplitz_mpo(siteinds(2, 3), Dict(-1 => 1.0, 0 => -2.0, 1 => 1.0), bc)
contract_mpo(A)  # 8 × 8 circulant second difference

ndim_operator_mpo(onedim_mpo::MPO, axis::Int, site_inds::Vector{<:Index}, arch::QTTArchitecture{N}) -> MPO

Return the $N$-dimensional operator that applies the one-dimensional QTT operator onedim_mpo to variable axis and the identity to every other variable.

The cores of onedim_mpo, given with the most significant bit first, go on the sites that carry axis, in the bit order of arch (reversed by reverse_mpo if var_nsites[axis] is negative), and identities go on the other sites.   • axis::Int: the variable.

  • arch::QTTArchitecture{N}: the layout.

Throws

  • DimensionMismatch: if site_inds does not have total_sites(arch) indices, or onedim_mpo does not have one site per bit of axis (in particular, if axis is not a variable of arch).

DifferenTT.ndim_operator_sum_mpo — Method
ndim_operator_sum_mpo(onedim_ops::NTuple{N,Any}, site_inds::Vector{<:Index}, arch::QTTArchitecture{N}) -> MPO

Return the Kronecker sum $\sum_{c=1}^{N} \mathbb{I} \otimes \cdots \otimes A_c \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathbb{I}$ of the one-dimensional QTT operators onedim_ops (one MPO per variable), as an exact MPO.

As for the TTArchitecture method, we write the sum directly instead of adding the lifted operators of ndim_operator_mpo. Its bonds carry the states of an automaton: state 1, no operator placed yet; state 2, one operator completed; and, for every variable whose sites lie on both sides of the bond, the bond of its operator while that operator is being placed. The bond dimension at bond $k$ is therefore $2 + \sum_c \chi_c(k)$, where the sum runs over these variables and $\chi_c(k)$ is the bond dimension of $A_c$ at that point: $2 + \chi_c$ inside the block of a variable in a blocked layout, and up to $2 + \sum_c \chi_c$ in an interleaved one. Reversed variables are handled as in ndim_operator_mpo, and variables with a single site complete their operator on that site.

Arguments

  • onedim_ops::NTuple{N,Any}: one MPO per variable, on its abs(var_nsites[c]) sites, most significant bit first.
  • site_inds::Vector{<:Index}: sites of the full chain, in site order (e.g. siteinds(arch)).
  • arch::QTTArchitecture{N}: the layout.

Throws

  • DimensionMismatch: if site_inds does not have total_sites(arch) indices.

DifferenTT.elementwise_function — Function
elementwise_function(func::Function, mps::MPS, positive_domain::Bool=true; cutoff=0.0, nsite=2,
                     maxdim=typemax(Int), oversample=15, outputlevel=0, rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng(),
                     kws...) -> MPS

Return an MPS approximation of the element-wise map func.(x) of the vector $\mathbf{x}$ represented by mps, built by recursive sketched interpolation (RSI), as in Sec. 3.2 of Torrente et al., Factorized approximate inverse preconditioners in the tensor train format.

A nonlinear function cannot be applied to a tensor train core by core, and forming the dense vector costs $\mathcal{O}(d^N)$. RSI avoids both: at every bond, from left to right, it contracts the sites to the right of the current block of nsite sites with random vectors (a sketch), which leaves a small array of size $\chi \times d^{\text{nsite}} \times k$ on which func can be applied entry by entry. An interpolative decomposition of the mapped array (interpolative) then gives the next core of the result, and mps is re-expressed on the same pivots before moving on (Fig. 5). The sketches of every suffix are built once, in one right-to-left pass, as in _rsi_tt_mul (suffix_sketches), so the sketching costs $\mathcal{O}(N k d \chi^2)$ for $k$ = oversample $+ \chi$ sketch columns, and each block then costs $\mathcal{O}(d^2 \chi^2 k)$ to contract and map and $\mathcal{O}(d^3 \chi k \min(\chi, k))$ to decompose. With fewer than nsite sites, func is applied to the full tensor instead.

The original RSI draws Gaussian sketches, whose contraction with a positive tensor train can have entries of any sign. For functions defined only on positive arguments, such as the $x^{-1/2}$ that gives the rescaling $D$ of the FAI, we draw uniform sketches in $[0, 1)$ instead (positive_domain = true), so that the sketched entries of an entrywise-positive mps stay positive (Sec. 3.2). In that case the pivot columns are also fitted with a pseudo-inverse. With positive_domain = false they are fitted by C \ M, which throws a SingularException for a part of the Gaussian draws (about half of them for exp of a signed function on $2^8$ points in our tests); the result is approximate in both cases, with an accuracy set by the sketch size.

Arguments

  • func::Function: scalar function applied to every entry.
  • mps::MPS: the vector $\mathbf{x}$; it must be entrywise positive if positive_domain is true and func is only defined for positive arguments.
  • positive_domain::Bool=true: draw uniform, positive sketches instead of Gaussian ones.

Keywords

  • cutoff=0.0: absolute pivot threshold of the interpolative decompositions (prrldu).
  • nsite=2: number of sites decomposed at a time.
  • maxdim=typemax(Int): maximum number of pivots, i.e. bond dimension, of every decomposition.
  • oversample=15: sketch columns on top of the bond dimension of mps.
  • outputlevel=0: print the ranks of the result if at least 1.
  • rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng(): random number generator of the sketches.

Other keywords are ignored.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if nsite < 2.

Examples

using StableRNGs
D = elementwise_function(x -> inv(sqrt(x)), diag_mps, true; cutoff=1e-12, rng=StableRNG(1))

The recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm is that of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire, arXiv:2602.17974. Our implementation started from a rough implementation that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.

DifferenTT.tt_mul — Method
tt_mul(mps1::MPS, mps2::MPS; cutoff::Float64=1e-15, maxdim::Int=typemax(Int64),
       rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng(), rsi_kwargs...) -> MPS
tt_mul(mpo1::MPO, mpo2::MPO; cutoff::Float64=1e-15, maxdim::Int=typemax(Int64)) -> MPO
tt_mul(mps::MPS, mpo::MPO; cutoff::Float64=1e-15, maxdim::Int=typemax(Int64)) -> MPO
tt_mul(mpo::MPO, mps::MPS; cutoff::Float64=1e-15, maxdim::Int=typemax(Int64)) -> MPO

Return the element-wise (Hadamard) product of two tensor trains on the same sites and array backend, truncated to the relative Frobenius cutoff and to maxdim.

Element-wise products appear wherever a nonlinear or variable-coefficient term is evaluated in the TT format, such as the advection term of the Navier–Stokes equations. Their exact bond dimension is the product $\chi_1 \chi_2$ of those of the factors, although the result usually needs far less. For two MPS we therefore use the recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) cross of _rsi_tt_mul, whose raw rank is bounded by its sketch size, and compress its non-orthogonal output once by SVD. Both steps matter: truncating inside the cross is far from optimal at a given rank, and the raw output carries two to four times the rank that the product needs, which every later apply, sum and time-stepper stage would pay for. If either factor has a zero tensor (iszero_tt), the zero MPS is returned. If the cross throws or returns a non-finite result, the product is computed by _direct_tt_mul instead, and the fallback is counted by tt_mul_fallbacks.

Note the limits of this choice. The cross is accurate only if the rank of the product fits in its sketch: when it does not, the result can be far off without any error being raised (see _rsi_tt_mul, and pass target_rank). Nor is it always faster: on $2^{18}$ to $2^{20}$ points, with smooth factors of bond dimension 5 to 10, or with one factor of bond dimension 2 and the other of 16 to 64, the direct product was 5 to 12 times faster in our tests.

The products that involve an MPO are computed exactly and then truncated. For two MPO, every site is contracted with delta tensors that identify the indices of both, which gives the entrywise product $(A \circ B)_{ij} = A_{ij} B_{ij}$. For an MPS $\mathbf{x}$ and an MPO $M$, in either order, the result is $\mathrm{Diagonal}(\mathbf{x}) M$, i.e. row $i$ of $M$ scaled by $x_i$.

Keywords

  • cutoff::Float64=1e-15: relative Frobenius cutoff of the final truncation.
  • maxdim::Int=typemax(Int64): maximum bond dimension of the result.
  • rng::AbstractRNG=Random.default_rng() (MPS-MPS only): random number generator of the sketches.
  • rsi_kwargs... (MPS-MPS only): passed to _rsi_tt_mul, e.g. oversample, target_rank and pivot_rtol.

Throws

  • ArgumentError (products with an MPO): if the site indices or the array backends differ.

The recursive sketched interpolation (RSI) algorithm is that of Meng, Khoo, Li & Stoudenmire, arXiv:2602.17974. Our implementation started from a rough implementation that its authors shared with us by private communication, and that they agreed to have published.
